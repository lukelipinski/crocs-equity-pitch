# Crocs (CROX) Equity Pitch: Buy, $137 Target

Independent equity research project on Crocs, Inc. (NASDAQ: CROX). **Recommendation: BUY**, 12-month target price **$137**, +14% vs the $120.18 close on 19 October 2026.

## Files
- **Crocs Stock Pitch - Comps.xlsx**: comparable-company model (inputs in blue, formulas in black)
- **Crocs Stock Pitch - Write-up.pdf**: 2-page recommendation

## How I chose the company
A rules-based screen: 246 brands I use, then 63 listed parent companies, then financial checks (profitable, minimum size), takeover checks and peer checks (3–5 valid peers), then scoring. Crocs had the clearest case.

## Valuation
- **Peers:** Birkenstock, Deckers, VF Corp, Nike, Adidas (all data from the same day, 19 Oct 2026)
- **Route A, forward P/E:** peer-median forward P/E x Crocs forward EPS = $177.22
- **Route B, EV/EBITDA:** (peer-median EV/EBITDA x Crocs EBITDA - net debt) / shares = $144.15
- **Blend:** average of the two = $160.68
- **Discount:** 15%, justified by Crocs' own history (it traded 43–56% below peers on forward P/E at recent quarter-ends) = **$136.58, rounded to $137**
- **Cross-checks:** implied ~9.2x forward earnings, the top of Crocs' own one-year range (6.6x–9.2x); close to the analyst average target of $138.75

## Thesis
1. **Core Crocs brand re-accelerating:** growth from +1.5% (2025) to +4.3% (Q2 2026), led by direct-to-consumer and international
2. **HEYDUDE bottoming out:** decline narrowed from -13.3% to -5.7%, with a $737M impairment already taken
3. **Buybacks at a low valuation:** a ~$2.0B remaining authorisation (about a third of market cap), compounding EPS at ~8x forward earnings

## Key risks
HEYDUDE fails to stabilise · tariffs and margins · Crocs brand fades · leverage · value trap

## Why comparables, not a DCF?
The thesis is about relative value, and comparables use today's market prices rather than long-range forecasts. A DCF cross-check is a planned next step.

## What I got wrong (and fixed)
An early figure suggested ~31% EPS growth, but it compared GAAP trailing earnings with adjusted forward earnings. On a like-for-like basis growth was ~7–12%, so I toned down the thesis.
